[ Kandinskia ]

Scientific Name:

Plasmalemma Kandinskia

Discovered by / at:

Discovered by Amy Park, in Kandinsky’s art studio while he was mixing paints and experimenting with abstract form. March 6 2026

Family:

Other Membrane based bacteria cells. Specifically, part of the Mycoplasma organisms which known for their flexible membranes and unusual cellular structures, where internal components float and reorganize within the cell.

Habitat:

A vibrant, ever shifting chromatic ecosystem where color behaves like energy and shapes float like living cells.

Appearance:

A soft, membrane-like organism filled with orbiting geometric shapes such as circles, rectangles, and triangles inspired by Kandinsky’s abstract compositions. Its body pulses, stretches, and morphs with color fluctuations with the paintbrush.

Key Characteristics:

  • Its inner geometric organs orbit and react independently.
  • Its color shifts dynamically when pressed.
  • It exhibits a behavior, expanding when approached through different directions.
  • Its membrane can be colored using a palette and paintbrush.
  • Each regeneration of the background is different due to randomness.

What we know so far about Kandinskia? Kandinskia is a generative chromatic organism that feeds on movement and creative energy. It reacts to proximity and touch by altering the structure of its membrane and the colors within its body. Inside the membrane exists a unique inner life, where diverse geometric shapes orbit and move independently. Since it was discovered in an artist’s studio, the creature is believed to carry traces of artistic DNA. Because of this, its membrane can be painted using colors from a palette, which the organism absorbs and reflects through its internal forms. It can also randomly shift its colors when the creature is gently tickled. The creature’s body continuously floats across the canvas, producing soft pulsations that resemble the movement of a painter’s brushstroke.
Guidelines for human interaction with this creature Because Kandinskia carries traces of an artist’s DNA, only those willing to think like an artist can truly interact with the creature. Humans interact with Kandinskia using a paintbrush cursor. By pressing and dragging the brush across the canvas, users can gently distort the creature’s membrane and influence the colors and shapes within it. The direction of movement affects how the membrane expands, while sustained pressure strengthens the transformation of its internal colors. As the paintbrush feeds color into the organism, the geometric shapes living inside the membrane absorb and redistribute these pigments, causing the creature’s body to shift and glow with new combinations of color. Users can also extract colors directly from the palette. When a color on the palette is pressed, the paintbrush carries that pigment to the creature, allowing the membrane to be painted in the selected color. In this way, users are able to “paint” directly onto the creature’s surface.
